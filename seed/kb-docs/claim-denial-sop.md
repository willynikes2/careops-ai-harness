## §1 Purpose

This procedure gives CareOps billing staff a consistent way to review denied claims and record the next action. All examples and payer rules in this demonstration are synthetic. Use the assigned claim record and the relevant payer manual together. Do not infer missing facts from a similar claim or treat a suggested action as completed work.

## §2 Triage

Triage denials within **1 business day**. Read the denial code and reason, verify the claim identifier, and check the amount and service date. Identify the type of missing evidence or review needed before choosing a follow-up task. The initial note should make the next step clear enough for another staff member to continue the work.

Check payer deadlines during triage. A task's internal due date does not replace a payer's correction, authorization, or appeal deadline. Escalate uncertainty rather than silently assuming that a deadline has not passed.

## §3 Authorization denials (CO-197)

For authorization denials with code **CO-197**, create an **AUTH_DOCUMENTATION** follow-up task, **attach authorization records, then resubmit**. The task note should identify the documentation to obtain. Confirm that the record supports the intended resubmission before changing claim status.

For Payer A, also follow §4.2 Prior Authorization in its provider manual: obtain authorization documentation, submit a retro-authorization request within **30 days** of the denial, then resubmit. A task creation alone does not complete this sequence or change the payer's decision.

## §4 Coding denials

For coding denials, create a **CODING_REVIEW** task. Describe the coding question in a one-sentence note and route the supporting record through the billing workflow. Review the correction before resubmission. Avoid changing unrelated claim details while resolving a coding issue.

## §5 Escalation

Claims over **$5,000** are escalated to the **billing supervisor**. Include the claim identifier, denial reason, applicable deadline, and work already recorded when requesting review. Escalation adds oversight; it does not remove payer requirements or authorize a direct move to a paid status. Keep the claim's follow-up record current after the supervisor identifies the next step.
